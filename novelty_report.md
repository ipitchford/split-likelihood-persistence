# Prior-art and novelty record (dated 1 November 2026)

Target: Pace, Salvan & Sartori (2023), Canadian J. Statistics 51:877-896, doi 10.1002/cjs.11749, Section 3 conjecture
(asymptotic persistence of the split-naive sequence). The conjecture and the definitions were checked against the
publisher's open-access full text (CC BY 4.0; Early View HTML of 9 December 2022).

## Searches
Searched: Semantic Scholar (relevance search, citations of Pace et al. 2023 and of Pace & Salvan 2020; snippet search was
rate-limited), arXiv API (17 query families, e.g. "universal inference" AND sequential / anytime / "optional stopping";
"split likelihood ratio"; "running MLE"; "e-process" AND "universal inference"), OpenAlex, and general web search (standard
and extended). Full-text greps were run over 20 related arXiv papers. Independent searches by the reviewers found nothing
further.

## Findings
- No earlier refutation; no earlier statement of the constants (sqrt5-1)/2, c(r) or 1/K, or of the OU failure exponents.
- Closest prior statements:
  - Ramdas & Wang (2025, Sec. 7.7) remark without proof that split LR e-variables are not e-processes; their Sec. 7.9 gives
    the time-mixture repair, credited in Proposition 5A.
  - Wasserman et al. (2020, Sec. 8) require sequential updating "in a particular fashion" (running MLE).
  - Pace et al. themselves observed finite-horizon anti-conservativeness at nominal 80%.
- Classical tools credited: Hartman-Wintner, Strassen, Hewitt-Savage, Ville, Robbins, Darling-Robbins, Howard et al.
  stitching, Kolmogorov-Erdos-Feller, Vovk-Wang. The 3/2 logloglog boundary term is classical, recently re-derived by
  Balsubramani (2026).
- Evidence Press corpus: no release on universal inference, e-values or confidence sequences.

A bounded search is not proof of priority.
